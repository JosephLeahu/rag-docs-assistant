# How Volcanoes Are Monitored

## Seismic monitoring
Seismometers detect earthquakes caused by rising magma and fluid movement. Swarms of small earthquakes,
changes in their depth, and long-period or tremor signals can indicate magma on the move. Seismic data are
the backbone of volcano monitoring and are usually transmitted in real time.

## Ground deformation
GNSS (GPS) stations measure millimeter-scale movement of the ground surface. Tiltmeters measure tiny changes
in slope. Satellite radar interferometry (InSAR) maps deformation over large areas. Swelling of a volcano
can indicate magma accumulating beneath it.

## Volcanic gas
Scientists measure gases such as sulfur dioxide (SO2), carbon dioxide (CO2), and hydrogen sulfide (H2S),
from the ground, from aircraft, and from satellites. Increases in SO2 emission often accompany magma
rising close to the surface.

## Other methods
Webcams and thermal cameras watch for visual and temperature changes. Infrasound sensors detect
low-frequency sound from explosions. Hydrologic monitoring tracks changes in streams and springs.

## Combining data
No single method is definitive. Forecasts are based on combining multiple data streams and comparing
them with the volcano's past behavior and background levels.
