# Volcanic Ash Hazards

## What ash is
Volcanic ash consists of tiny fragments of rock and volcanic glass less than 2 millimeters across. Unlike
soft ash from a wood fire, it is hard, abrasive, and does not dissolve in water.

## Aviation
Ash is a major hazard to aircraft because it can melt inside jet engines and cause them to lose power. In
1982 a British Airways Boeing 747 lost all four engines after flying through ash from Galunggung volcano in
Indonesia, and in 1989 a KLM Boeing 747 lost all four engines in ash from Redoubt Volcano in Alaska. Both
crews restarted engines and landed safely. Nine Volcanic Ash Advisory Centers worldwide track ash clouds
for aviation.

## On the ground
Ash can irritate eyes and lungs, contaminate water supplies, damage vehicles and electronics, and disrupt
power. Ash is heavy when wet, so thick accumulations can collapse roofs.

## Preparing for ashfall
Recommended steps include keeping N95 masks and goggles on hand, staying indoors during ashfall, keeping
ash out of buildings, avoiding driving, and clearing ash from roofs carefully once it stops falling.
