# USGS Volcano Alert Levels and Aviation Color Codes

## Ground-based alert levels
The USGS uses four Volcano Alert Levels to communicate hazards on the ground.
NORMAL means the volcano is in a typical background, non-eruptive state.
ADVISORY means the volcano shows elevated unrest above known background levels.
WATCH means unrest is heightened or escalating, or a minor eruption is underway that poses limited hazards.
WARNING means a hazardous eruption is imminent, underway, or suspected.

## Aviation Color Codes
Because volcanic ash is dangerous to aircraft, a separate four-color code is used for aviation.
GREEN corresponds to a non-eruptive background state.
YELLOW indicates elevated unrest above background.
ORANGE indicates heightened unrest with increased likelihood of eruption, or an eruption underway with no or minor ash emission.
RED indicates an eruption is imminent or underway with significant emission of ash into the atmosphere.

## Who issues them
Alert levels and color codes are issued by the responsible USGS volcano observatory. For volcanoes in
Washington, Oregon, and Idaho that is the Cascades Volcano Observatory. Changes are distributed through
Volcano Activity Notices (VANs) and Volcano Observatory Notices for Aviation (VONAs).
