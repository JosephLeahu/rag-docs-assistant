# The 1980 Eruption of Mount St. Helens

## Build-up
After 123 years of quiet, Mount St. Helens reawakened in March 1980 with earthquakes and steam explosions.
Magma intruding into the volcano caused the north flank to bulge outward at a rate of roughly
1.5 meters (5 feet) per day.

## May 18, 1980
At 8:32 a.m. PDT on May 18, 1980, a magnitude 5.1 earthquake triggered the collapse of the bulging north
flank, producing the largest landslide in recorded history. The sudden release of pressure caused a lateral
blast that devastated about 600 square kilometers (230 square miles) of forest. An eruption column rose
roughly 24 kilometers (80,000 feet) and ash fell across eleven states.

## Impacts
Fifty-seven people were killed. The summit elevation dropped from 2,950 meters (9,677 feet) to
2,549 meters (8,363 feet), leaving a horseshoe-shaped crater. Lahars (volcanic mudflows) traveled down the
Toutle and Cowlitz Rivers, destroying homes and bridges and clogging the Columbia River shipping channel.

## Later activity
Lava dome growth continued intermittently through 1986. A second dome-building eruption occurred from
2004 to 2008. Mount St. Helens remains the most active volcano in the Cascade Range.
